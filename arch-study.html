Decision, 2026-11-06. Modeled as option B with a bigger drop: the whole arch sits 13 in lower than drawn here, its feet on flat 2x4 sills lying on the ground at the bed centrelines, each sill staked at both panel edges. The inside corner posts are uncut 8 ft 2x4s set 20 in deep, so they stand 6 ft 4 in above grade, level with the crown. Headroom becomes about 6 ft 4 in at the walkway centre and 6 ft 2 in at the walls. The BOM page carries the built numbers.

Does it make sense?

Yes. Starting the arch at the middle of bed A and ending it at the middle of bed B gives a 6 ft 3 in spread, and a 16 ft panel over that spread stands about 6 ft 3 in above its feet. The two inside corner posts sit 30 in apart, right under the crown, so they are the natural place to hold the panel up: it crosses each post face only 4 in below the crown, where the panel is nearly flat. Screw the edge wire to the post face there with washers and the arch is held at the top as well as at the feet.

  • The posts must be taller than an 8 ft stick allows if the feet sit on the soil. The panel crosses the posts 7 ft 2 in above grade and you want about 4 in of post above the screws, so the post needs to stand 7 ft 5 in tall plus 9.5 in in the ground: a 10 ft 2x4 cut to 8 ft 2.5 in. Push the panel feet 6 in into the bed soil instead and an uncut 8 ft 2x4 (7 ft 2 in above grade) has 6 in of post above the crossing.
  • How the feet are held hardly matters at this spacing. Feet that can tilt (set on a sill, screwed with washers) give 6 ft 3 in above the feet and lean 6° outward at the bottom. Feet clamped upright in the soil lift the crown only 1.1 in more and bow the sides out 0.4 in. Both are drawn below; the posts are sized for the lower one so the screws land in wood either way.
  • Only the near edge of the panel is on the posts. The panel is 60.5 in wide and runs down the beds from the corner posts; its far edge is held only by its feet. A bent panel is stiff across its width, so that is fine for a trellis, and the first inside mid-span posts (41 in down bed A, 49 in down bed B) can be extended the same way later if it ever racks.
  • The feet want a sill, not loose soil. With free feet the panel pushes its ends outward; a flat 2x4 sill across each bed on its centreline (buried flush, 48 in long between the side boards) with the bottom wire screwed to it holds that, and it keeps the two feet exactly 6 ft 3 in apart so the arch comes out the height this page predicts.

Height against foot spacing, 16 ft panel

The sketch plots a catenary, which is the shape of a hanging chain. A galvanized panel is a stiff rod, not a chain, so its real shape is an elastica: the two solid curves. The catenary tracks the free-feet panel closely at wide spacings and overstates it at narrow ones, where a real panel bows out at the sides instead of rising further. Either way

  • Two tall posts per arch instead of four: bed A's inside corner post and bed B's inside corner post at the near end. The six other trellis posts from the previous design go back to being 24 in stakes and 14.5 in corner posts.
  • Edge wire to each post face with 2 × #9 2.5 in screws and 1 in fender washers at the crossing; bottom wire to each sill with 3 screws and washers.
  • The panel feet stay inside the beds, so nothing lands on the pavers and the walkway keeps its full 24 in.
  • Arch BC at the far end is not part of this study; it can be moved to the same scheme (bed B centre to bed C centre) or dropped.

Method

A bent cattle panel is a uniform elastic rod, so its centreline obeys the elastica equation (the pendulum equation in the tangent angle). Each curve is found by integrating that equation from the crown with a fourth-order Runge–Kutta scheme and shooting on the axial force until the chord comes out at the chosen spacing; the panel length then fixes the scale. Free feet means zero bending moment at the ends (pinned); held upright means the ends are forced vertical (clamped), which below 7 ft 4 in apart makes the sides bow outward before they rise. The pinned case was checked against its closed form in complete elliptic integrals (agreement within 0.05 in). The catenary is the hanging-chain curve of the same length, for comparison with the sketch. Self-weight and the panel's mesh are ignored; panel length 16 ft and bed geometry come from the Fusion extract.
