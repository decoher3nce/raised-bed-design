Plan

Four beds in a 24 ft x 18 ft plot. Side boards run outside the end boards, which are 48 in (21 in for Bed D). Bed A uses one board per course. Beds B, C and D use two boards per course from 8 ft stock (Bed B course 1: 96 + 48 in, course 2: 48 + 96 in; Bed C course 1: 96 + 72 in, course 2: 72 + 96 in; Bed D course 1: 96 + 96 in, course 2: 96 + 96 in), meeting over 2x8 joint posts: 24 in pieces of 2x8 lying flat with the 7 1/4 in face against the inside of the wall, 9.5 in in the ground like a mid-span post. Bed B's 48 in side pieces are cut from boards whose offcut is an end board; Bed C's 72 in side pieces are cut from boards whose offcut is a joint post. Beds B and C stagger the seams course to course, so no joint runs the full height of the wall. 2x4 posts stand inside each corner, edge-on to the side boards, and lie flat against the long walls every 24 in, measured from the near end (where two side boards meet, a 2x8 joint post stands in for the stake), where they run 24 in long and are driven 9.5 in into the ground. The hog-wire arch between beds A and B stands on 2x4 sills at the bed centrelines and is held near its crown by the two inside corner posts at the near end, uncut 8 ft 2x4s set 20 in deep. Letters follow the arch names in the model: Arch AB spans beds A and B; Arch BC is modeled but parked. 6 low-tunnel hoops of 1 in tube (the blue bars) cover Bed C at 31 in pitch, the first and last 5 in inside the end boards.

Screw plan

Every joint is a 2x8 board face-screwed to a 2x4 post: 2 screws per board per post, 1.75 in and 5.5 in up from the bottom edge of each board. Corner posts stand edge-on, 1.5 in edge against the side board and 3.5 in face against the end board, so the side-board screws there drive into 3.5 in of post. Mid-span posts lie flat, 3.5 in face against the side board, and run 24 in long so 9.5 in is driven into the ground; the screws into them are shorter and staggered across the face. Where two side boards meet over a 2x8 joint post (beds B, C and D), each board end gets its own pair, 1.75 in from the end.

Which lower riser to buy

Measure the main's depth at each bed before buying the lower risers. The real depth of the existing main is not known: the 6 1/2 in drawn here is only what an 8 in lower riser gives. The rule: hole centre above grade = lower riser + upper riser + 3 3/8 in minus the depth of the main's centre below grade. The 3 3/8 in is what the reducing elbow, the valve and the top elbow add once the threads are made up. With the 6 in upper riser, a lower riser 1 1/2 in longer than the main is deep puts the hole in the middle of the top board.

The hole may sit anywhere from 9 5/8 to 13 1/4 in above grade: any lower and the valve's bottom face is less than 1/2 in above grade, any higher and the hole's centre is less than 1 1/4 in from the top board's upper edge.
