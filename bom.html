Plan

Four beds in a 24 ft x 18 ft plot. Side boards run outside the end boards, which are 48 in (21 in for Bed D). Beds A and B use one board per course. Beds C and D use two boards per course from 8 ft stock (Bed C course 1: 96 + 72 in, course 2: 72 + 96 in; Bed D course 1: 96 + 96 in, course 2: 96 + 96 in), meeting over 2x8 joint posts: 24 in pieces of 2x8 lying flat with the 7 1/4 in face against the inside of the wall, 9.5 in in the ground like a mid-span post. Bed C's joint posts are the offcuts of its 72 in side pieces. Bed C staggers the seams course to course, so no joint runs the full height of the wall. 2x4 posts stand inside each corner, edge-on to the side boards, and lie flat against the long walls at mid-span, where they run 24 in long and are driven 9.5 in into the ground. The hog-wire arch between beds A and B stands on 2x4 sills at the bed centrelines and is held near its crown by the two inside corner posts at the near end, uncut 8 ft 2x4s set 20 in deep. Letters follow the arch names in the model: Arch AB spans beds A and B; Arch BC is modeled but parked. 6 low-tunnel hoops of 1 in tube (the blue bars) cover Bed C at 31 in pitch, the first and last 5 in inside the end boards.

Screw plan

Every joint is a 2x8 board face-screwed to a 2x4 post: 2 screws per board per post, 1.75 in and 5.5 in up from the bottom edge of each board. Corner posts stand edge-on, 1.5 in edge against the side board and 3.5 in face against the end board, so the side-board screws there drive into 3.5 in of post. Mid-span posts lie flat, 3.5 in face against the side board, and run 24 in long so 9.5 in is driven into the ground; the screws into them are shorter and staggered across the face. Where two side boards meet over a 2x8 joint post (beds C and D), each board end gets its own pair, 1.75 in from the end.

Buy 3 in: 136 needed, one 5 lb box (about 350); 1 lb boxes hold only about 70. Buy 2.5 in: 168 needed, one 5 lb box (about 450); 1 lb boxes hold only about 90. Use #9 star-drive exterior structural screws, coated for treated lumber or stainless 305 or 316 for cedar and redwood, which stain and corrode plain coated screws. Pre-drill the corner ties and any screw within 2 in of a board end (1/8 in bit for cedar, 3/32 in for SPF).

Set the mid-span posts and the joint posts first: chamfer the bottom of each to a point, drive it 9 1/2 in at the marked centerline with its wide face toward the wall, and stop when its top is flush with the top of the wall. Then screw the side boards to it from outside. Corner posts stay 14.5 in and sit on grade, except the 2 inside corner posts at the near end of beds A and B under the arch, which are uncut 8 ft trellis posts set 20 in deep (see the arch).

Each bed's plan under Materials by bed is the cheapest set of sticks for that bed on its own, so the four plans add up to this table. Side boards are whole sticks; the 72 in side pieces are cut from 8 ft boards and the offcut becomes a 2x8 joint post, cut up to 1 in short so both clear the kerf. End boards may be cut up to 1/4 in short so two 48 in pieces clear the saw kerf on one 8 ft board; corner posts may run up to 1/2 in short of the wall height and mid-span posts, joint posts and arch stakes up to 1 in short, which only takes them a little less deep into the ground, when that lets one more piece fit a stick.
